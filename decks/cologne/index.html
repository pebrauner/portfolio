Cologne Major Coverage Plan.

An editorial proposal for rdy.gg's coverage of the 24th CS Major, returning to Cologne after ten years. The cathedral was always going to be the room. The question is what we put on the walls.

Event IEM Cologne Major 2026
Dates 02 — 21 Jun 2026
Venue LANXESS Arena, Köln
Prize Pool USD 1.25M
By The rdy.gg content team
▌ THE FRAMING / WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

This document is a working proposal, not a directive. It's a collaboration across the rdy.gg content team. Treat every pillar below as a starting point, open to cuts, swaps, additions, or full rejections. The intent is to put the full shape of what we could do in one room, so the team can argue about it productively.

Cologne is the 24th CS Major and the first one back at LANXESS in a decade. The audience that shows up will be older, louder, and more knowledgeable than at any other event on the 2026 calendar. They have seen everything we are about to film. The framing we owe them is editorial gravitas without the pageantry. Coverage that reads like a record, not a recap.

What follows is structured liturgically because the venue invites it and the joke writes itself. The intent underneath is dry. Sections are numbered. Owners are named. Conditional pieces are marked. Anything we cut goes in the colophon so we remember the call. Every deliverable on the page can be marked approved, maybe, or ruled out using the three buttons at the bottom of its card. Ideas added during the meeting become deliverables on the same level. The sidebar will count whatever ends up approved and export those.

The cathedral was always going to be the room. The question is what we put on the walls.
— Working epigraph · Cologne Coverage v1
I
Section One / Pre-event
I.

Prelude

What we ship before the doors open. Front-loading the read so the on-site work has somewhere to point.

Pillars: 6 Owners: Elliott · Sam May → 01 Jun

Six pre-event pillars, all remote, all written. Three bespoke to Cologne, three pulled from the rdy.gg segments menu. The supercomputer becomes a recurring asset through the event. The framing piece exists in two forms, one to read and one to film.

01 / 06
Owner: Elliott Remote Series

Team breakdowns

The top ten to fifteen teams get a dedicated article each. Lower-ranked teams aggregated three to four per piece. Every breakdown includes strengths, weaknesses, and an rdy.gg diagnostic, the editorial take that's the reason a reader comes to us instead of HLTV.

Volume target: roughly fifteen pieces across the slate, paced over two weeks. Headlines should land in the same voice across the set so the series reads as one body.

02 / 06
Owner: Sam Remote Recurring asset

Supercomputer, pre-major edition

Machine learning predictions on stage advancement and Diamond Coin standings. First publish moment of the event. This piece becomes a returning fixture through the tournament, refreshed before Stage 2 and again before playoffs.

Reference our prior supercomputer pieces for tone and visual treatment. The pre-major edition is the most read of the three, because nobody else is putting up numbers yet.

03 / 06
Owners: Elliott, camera op Remote + on-site Two formats

Why this Cologne is different

The Major-versus-IEM framing piece. Soft push to Elliott for the article version: 1,200 to 1,500 words, history-aware but not nostalgic. The on-site vox-pop version is filmed during the event, audience answering the same question in their own words. The two halves can publish a few days apart.

04 / 06
Owners: Sam + Elliott Remote Segments menu Ranking

AWP Hierarchy

Pre-major ranking of the top AWPers heading into Cologne. Stats, impact, consistency. One AWPer can decide a Bo3 at this stage; the piece exists because readers expect to see who's hot before brackets lock.

05 / 06
Owners: Sam + Elliott Remote Segments menu Per top team Tactical

Map Pool Report

One report per top team. Strengths, weaknesses, permabans, opponent targets. Sits alongside the team breakdowns as the tactical spine, written so the Stage 1 recaps can reference it without re-explaining ban orders.

06 / 06
Owners: Sam + Elliott Remote Segments menu Stakes · recurring

Major Pressure Tracker

Track which teams are fighting for Cologne spots and Valve points. Refreshed as Stage results land. Cologne is the Major itself, so the stakes board reads twice: who clears, and who fails to clear and falls out of the running.

Section Two / During the event
II.

The Service

From the first map of Stage 1 to the grand final trophy lift. The bulk of the work, the bulk of the publish.

Pillars: 9 On-site lead: Horizzon (Stage 2 →) 02 → 21 Jun
i.Stage 1 / The Read from Lima
Owner: Elliott Remote only

Stage 1 recaps

Elliott writes the Stage 1 recaps from his desk. No on-site presence until Stage 2. The pace is high, the camera crew has not arrived, the supercomputer has not refreshed. Output is editorial, written, daily.

ii.Stage 2 → Playoffs / Nine pillars

Each pillar below is named. Each has an owner. Volume discipline applies, especially on mixed-zone. Not every player needs a clip.

01 / 09
Owner: Sam Remote ×2 drops

Supercomputer refreshes

Two publish moments. One before Stage 2 begins. One before playoffs. Each is its own headline asset, not a small update post.

02 / 09
Owner: Horizzon On-site Priority push

Mixed-zone interviews

Audio recorded for every interview. Pedro publishes to Spotify from Lima, embeds widget on every related piece. The positioning stance: rdy.gg leans into Stage 2 orgs that rarely get camera time, the MIBR-type teams other outlets skip past for the default Vitality clip. Tier-1 standard coverage still applies for Vitality, Falcons, FURIA, Spirit.

03 / 09
Owner: Horizzon On-site Named pillar

Coach interviews

Coaches are more articulate, more strategic, and more available than players. B1ad3, dastan, zonic, the German coaches in the home market are examples, not a fixed list. Aim for scheduled sit-downs across the event. Post-elimination coaches give the strongest material.

04 / 09
Owner: Horizzon On-site Soft commit

Post-elimination lane

Longer-form sit-down request when a team is eliminated and before they fly home. Different question set than mixed zone. Soft commitment, not a hard floor. Some teams will say no. If we miss them, it is not a missed deliverable.

05 / 09
Brokered by Sam On-site 10 — 15 min

Talent interviews

Sam brokers, probable yes but not contractual. German casters in the home market are the angle. Ten to fifteen minute sit-downs. Talent is not obligated to us and can decline.

06 / 09
Owner: Horizzon On-site High priority 60 — 90s recurring

Supercomputer reaction segment

Horizzon shows players Sam's predictions on camera, films the reaction. Also works as vox-pop with fans on the floor. Low production cost, recurring across the event, high audience pull. The format that pays for itself.

07 / 09
Owner: Horizzon + camera op On-site Social-first

Audience vox-pops

Brief social edits. Doubles as the live half of the "Why this Cologne is different" piece. The Cologne audience is opinionated and on camera by choice. They are also the readymade context for everything else we ship.

08 / 09
Owner: Camera op On-site Proven, from Rio

Atmospheric B-roll

A dedicated daily window for the camera op. Carved out of the schedule, not squeezed between other asks. Non-negotiable. Cologne and LANXESS specifically are high-value for this content: the crowd, the arches, the queues, the lights. The reel that travels furthest after the event.

09 / 09
Owner: Elliott Remote In production

Daily editorial recap

Already greenlit, already in motion. Listed here for completeness so we do not accidentally cannibalize its slot.

iii.From the segments menu / Format addition

One format pulled from the rdy.gg segments menu, reactive and daily. Lives alongside the nine pillars but pitched in a different register: short, fast, identifiable.

Owner: Elliott Remote Segments menu Daily · quick-turn

First Take

Short reactive piece after each major match or news beat. Pure rdy.gg perspective, no pretense of comprehensive coverage. One per match day at minimum, often two on the big days. Sits next to the daily recap as the faster sibling.

rdy.gg leans into less-covered teams while other outlets default to the top four. That is the position, and Cologne is where it shows.

III
Section Three / Conditional · Win Condition Live
III.

The Pilgrimage

A live or live-to-tape episode of Win Condition from Cologne. Big swing, real cost, open variables. Honest framing follows.

Status: proposal CONDITIONAL · NOT BUDGETED

Potentially the biggest piece of content rdy.gg has ever made. Also genuinely hard. Here is what it would take.

Decision A

Format fork

Two options, not three.

  • Option A. Live broadcast. Highest impact, highest production cost, highest failure surface.
  • Option B. Recorded on the day, posted within 24 to 48 hours. Most of the upside, most of the room to fix mistakes.
Decision B

Venue

Bar with live audience: ideal. Highest impact, highest budget. The format and the room speak to each other.

Hotel meeting room: ruled out. The vibe does not survive a hotel meeting room.

If live

Equipment list, minimum viable

  • Two cameras, switched.
  • Switcher for basic camera cuts.
  • One multi-input capture card if we're going live, so both feeds reach the PC for the broadcast. Plenty of options on the market. If we record on the day instead, footage stays on the cameras and gets edited later, no capture card needed.
  • One PC, for graphics and run-of-show.
  • Three to four broadcast-grade microphones, one per chair.
  • Rodecaster Pro or equivalent mixer. Non-optional.
  • Most of this can be rented locally in Köln; nothing here requires ownership.
Soft commits

Guests

Talent or players. Must be confirmed before we commit to a date. Sam's most likely on-camera moment, if he says yes. He is hesitant, not opposed.

▌ WORTH POINTING OUT

Things to plan around, not blockers.

  • Whoever runs it on the day should be on-site. Remote ops on a live podcast is doable but adds friction we don't need.
  • This is the kind of format that rewards overpreparation. Run-of-show, dry-run, contingencies on every guest and every cue.
  • Gear and venue can be sorted with local rentals and a couple of phone calls. None of it is a real obstacle.
  • If we can't staff it to the level above by the date, record on the day and post the cut. The fallback is still a strong piece.
Potentially the biggest piece of content rdy.gg has ever made. Also genuinely hard. The proposal is honest about both halves.
— III. The Pilgrimage / framing
IV
Section Four / Strategic direction
IV.

The Vision

A team mini-documentary series. Not a Cologne deliverable. A flag planted in the ground for where rdy.gg should be heading.

Status: direction, not commitment Pilot window: any team that bites
Format 20 minutes per episode Long-form

One episode per team, selected list

Twenty minutes per episode. Selected list, not a full roster. The intent is the emotional side of the players, the side rdy.gg does not usually show. Coach Arthur and voo-style depth, but as a series with a shared visual language and a release cadence.

Requires team alignment, schedule access, deeper relationships than mixed-zone gives us. Not something to launch cold at Cologne. But Cologne can be a pilot if a team bites and the access is real.

Why now

Framing for the team

This belongs in the deck because the rest of the deck is tactical. Cologne is a single tournament. The Vision is the answer to "what is rdy.gg in two years." Listing it here forces us to keep one eye there while we run the present.

V. Open Questions / Things that may or may not ship

Three pieces that are easy to suggest and worth flagging, with a ship-or-cut decision pushed to the meeting. The third is new since the last draft and may extend well beyond Cologne.

01 / 03
Owner: Camera op · social edit by anyone On-site Photo essay or montage

Banner and sign culture

German CS fan banners at LANXESS are their own art form. Either a photo essay or a 60-second montage. Camera op alignment needed early so the shots actually get captured. Easy to suggest, ship-or-cut later.

02 / 03
Owner: Elliott Remote Evergreen · slow content

Cologne Returns / Anchor piece

Why Cologne as a venue matters in CS history. LANXESS legacy, German CS culture, iconic past moments. Written by Elliott, depends on bandwidth. Indexable, slow content, the kind of piece that pulls SEO long after the event is over.

03 / 03
New in v1 Article + video essay Potential running series

Tournament Origins / Video essay series

An article plus an edited video essay for the rdy.gg YouTube channel on the origin story of a single tournament. Cologne is the pilot because the history is the deepest and most visual: a decade of LANXESS moments, the venue's relationship to CS as a sport, why this is the cathedral and not a metaphor.

If the pilot works, it becomes a recurring series across the rdy ecosystem. Each major and each long-running circuit stop gets an episode. Long-tail, evergreen, ours to own.

Recorded and published within 72 hours of the grand final. The post-mortem episode while the read is still live. Format consistent with the season slate.
